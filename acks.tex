\chapter*{Foreword and acknowledgments}

This text accompanies \textit{xv6++}, an object-oriented refactoring of
the xv6 teaching operating system.  The original xv6 text and kernel
were developed for operating systems courses at MIT by Russ Cox,
Frans Kaashoek, and Robert Morris.  Xv6 is modeled on Dennis
Ritchie's and Ken Thompson's Unix Version 6 (v6)~\cite{unix}.  It
loosely follows the structure and style of v6, but is implemented in
ANSI C~\cite{kernighan} for a multi-core RISC-V~\cite{riscv} system.

The xv6++ edition builds upon the xv6 RISC-V source and text,
reconstructing the kernel using modern C++ techniques in order to
explore object-oriented design, encapsulation, and modularity in
operating system implementation.  The original xv6 source and book are
available at \url{https://github.com/mit-pdos/xv6-riscv} and
\url{https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/6.1810}.

The xv6++ kernel and accompanying materials are available at
\url{https://github.com/Skylands-Research-Institute/xv6pp-riscv}.
This edition is used in COMS221 Operating Systems at Sussex County
Community College.

The original xv6 text has been used in MIT's 6.828 and 6.1810 operating
systems courses.  The original authors thank the faculty, teaching
assistants, and students of those classes who directly or indirectly
contributed to xv6.  In particular, they acknowledge Adam Belay,
Austin Clements, and Nickolai Zeldovich, as well as many individuals
who provided bug reports and suggestions for improvement.

The development of xv6++ was supported in part by a grant from the
Skylands Research Institute at Sussex County Community College.
The author gratefully acknowledges this support.

Any errors or omissions in this xv6++ edition are the responsibility
of its author.
